Growing & Wellbeing

Exploring how therapeutic engagement with living ecosystems transforms health, human biology and community connection.

Cultivating Wellbeing Through Nature

Green social prescribing connects individuals to nature-based activities—such as community gardening, seed saving and food growing—to actively support mental and physical health. Rather than relying solely on clinical frameworks, this approach integrates human health with environmental care through shared outdoor experiences.

The Science of the Soil

The restorative power of gardening is backed by a growing body of neuroscientific research. When we work with healthy, living earth, we naturally inhale and absorb a common soil bacterium called Mycobacterium vaccae.

Clinical and laboratory studies show that exposure to this microbe activates specific serotonin-producing brain neurons in the dorsal raphe nucleus, triggering a natural release of serotonin—the vital neurotransmitter responsible for regulating mood, decreasing anxiety and building emotional stability.

  • Natural Stress Resilience: Research demonstrates that these microscopic soil organisms act as powerful, natural mood-regulators, stimulating brain pathways in a manner structurally similar to antidepressant treatments.
  • Holistic Healing: By nurturing the soil city beneath our feet, we physically engage with a natural mood-boosting mechanism that leaves us feeling profoundly grounded, calmer and more connected.

Scientific Baseline Reference:

“Identification of an immune-responsive mesolimbocortical serotonergic system: Application of the soil-derived bacterium Mycobacterium vaccae triggers activation of serotonin-containing neurons within the dorsal raphe nucleus, increasing serotonin metabolism in the prefrontal cortex and producing antidepressant-like behavioral responses.”

— Dr Chris Lowry et al., Neuroscience Journal (University of Bristol & University College London)
